# Cold calling UK trades businesses: the rules to follow

**This is a practical guide, not legal advice.** The rules are the Privacy and Electronic Communications Regulations (PECR) and UK GDPR, enforced by the ICO, plus Ofcom rules on silent and abandoned calls. Check the current ICO guidance on "business-to-business marketing calls" before you start and whenever something is unclear. If in doubt, don't ring, or ask a solicitor.

## 1. Check every number before the first marketing call
- **Sole traders and ordinary partnerships** (many one-van trades) are treated like individuals. Check them against the **TPS** (Telephone Preference Service).
- **Limited companies, LLPs and public bodies** can register with the **CTPS** (Corporate TPS). Check them against the CTPS.
- If you can't tell which a business is, check both, or look the business up (Companies House shows limited companies).
- **Do not make a marketing call to a number on the TPS or CTPS** unless that person has told you, in a way you can show, that they are happy to hear from you.
- Checking numbers properly needs access to the TPS and CTPS lists (a licence or a checking service). Look up the current options and costs on the TPS and CTPS websites. Do not guess. Until you have a way to check, do not cold call.
- Write the result on the lead in HQ (Call plan, "TPS and CTPS check") with the date. A "Registered" result hides the Call button.

## 2. On every call
- Say who you are (Gio, Localdesk) and why you are ringing, at the start.
- Be honest about what you are selling and who you are. Do not pretend to be a customer or imply a relationship.
- If they say no, thank them and stop. If they say "don't ring me again", tick **do not call** on the lead straight away. HQ then clears every follow-up for that lead and hides the Call button. Keep that record: it is your suppression list.
- Do not call at unsociable hours. There is no single legal hours rule for these calls, so use sensible working hours for tradespeople (for example early evening or lunch, not late evening or weekends) and avoid anyone who asks you not to.
- Do not make calls that are silent or hang up on the person. Do not use automated or recorded marketing messages.

## 3. Your number
- Ring from a number that shows your caller ID and that people can ring back on. Do not hide your number. Do not use a number you can't answer.
- Give your name and a contact address or number if they ask.

## 4. Following up
- **No unsolicited email or text campaigns.** After a cold call, only send a message (text, WhatsApp, email) if the person agreed to hear from you that way, and keep it to what they agreed (for example a Zoom link). HQ drafts are never sent automatically and the lead page reminds you of this for people you rang.
- Zoom invitations and reminders only for people who agreed to a demo.
- Don't keep chasing someone who hasn't engaged. Two or three tries at most, then stop and note it.

## 5. Their data (UK GDPR)
- You may hold a business contact's name, business and number for B2B sales under legitimate interests, but only keep what you need and tell people how you use it if they ask. Your privacy policy should say this (it needs your name, address and ICO registration number: see the launch checklist).
- Be ready to delete a lead when asked. HQ has a "Delete this lead" button that removes their details for good.
- Do not put prospects' phone numbers in the public GitHub repository, in chats or in AI tools. Keep them in HQ only.
- If you record calls, tell the person at the start. Mia's calls say they may be recorded. Don't record your own sales calls unless you say so.

- [ ] A way to check TPS and CTPS is in place.
- [ ] You can ring out showing your number.
- [ ] You know your opening line and how you will handle "not interested" and "don't call me again".
