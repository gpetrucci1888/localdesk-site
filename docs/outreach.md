# Finding your first 5 founding clients

> **Marketing rules (UK PECR), read first**
> - **Phone calls are fine**, but first check the number isn't registered with the Telephone Preference Service (TPS) or Corporate TPS. Many sole traders register their mobiles. You can check numbers through a TPS checking service.
> - **Don't send unsolicited marketing texts or emails to sole traders or partnerships.** The law treats them like private individuals, so you need their consent first. Most tradespeople are sole traders.
> - Unsolicited marketing emails to **limited companies** at a business address are allowed, as long as you say who you are and give an easy way to opt out.
> - **Once someone asks you to send details** ("text me the link"), you can send them, because they've asked.
> - In person, by phone, and replying to public "recommend me a…" posts are the safest routes.

Ads take a few days to warm up. Talking to local businesses directly is free and often faster. Aim for **20–30 conversations this week**.

## Who to contact first

Businesses that **miss calls because their hands are full** and where **one job is worth a lot**:
- Roofers, plumbers, heating engineers, electricians, builders
- Garages and MOT centres
- Small salons and barbers (owner is with a client)
- Locksmiths, tree surgeons, landscapers

Where to find them: Google Maps ("plumber near Callington", etc.), Facebook community groups, Checkatrade, Rated People, local Facebook "recommend a tradesperson" posts, vans with phone numbers on.

**Best sign they need you:** you ring them and it goes to voicemail. That's your opener.

## Phone script (2 minutes)

> "Hi, is that [name]? It's Gio from Localdesk, I'm local, in [town]. Have you got two minutes?
>
> I'll be quick. I've set up an AI receptionist for trades. When you're up a ladder or on a job and can't get to the phone, it answers, takes the customer's name, number and what they need, and texts it straight to you. So you don't lose the job to whoever answers next.
>
> I'm taking on five founding clients locally. The setup fee's waived, and it comes with a free website. Would it be worth a 15-minute demo where you hear it answer as your business?"

**If they're interested:** book it there and then on your Calendly, or send the link by text.
**If they're busy:** "No worries. Can I text you a link so you can try it yourself? You can talk to it on our website." (Only send it if they say yes.)

## When you get their voicemail (best opener)

Leave this (don't follow up by text unless they've said it's okay):

> "Hi, it's Gio from Localdesk. I tried to call and got your voicemail, which is exactly why I'm calling. I set up AI receptionists that answer when you can't, take the details and text you. Give me a ring back, or try it yourself at localdeskai.co.uk. Cheers."

**Text to send only after they've asked for the link:**
> Hi [name], Gio from Localdesk, as promised. You can talk to the AI receptionist yourself at localdeskai.co.uk (press "Talk to it now"). Taking on 5 founding clients locally with free setup. Happy to do a quick demo whenever suits. Reply STOP and I won't message again.

## Handling common questions

| They say | You say |
|---|---|
| "I don't miss many calls." | "Most people don't know. Callers who get voicemail usually just ring someone else. Try the calculator on the site: even one missed job a month usually covers it." |
| "Customers want a real person." | "They want their problem sorted. It's honest that it's a virtual receptionist, takes everything down properly and you ring them back. Better than voicemail, which most people hang up on." |
| "£397 is a lot." | "One job pays for it, and it includes a website. Compared with a receptionist at £20k+ a year, it's a fraction. And it's only a 3-month minimum." |
| "What if it gets something wrong?" | "Every call is recorded and transcribed, and you get the details by text. It never promises times or prices. It just takes the details for you." |
| "I'll think about it." | "Of course. Shall I book a quick 15-minute demo for later in the week, so you can hear it answer as your business?" |
